CORE PATTERNS

Start with the account story.

Good entries become easier when you ask what came in, what went out, what was earned, and who is owed.

Capital introduced

Trace the increase in cash or bank to the owner’s capital account and see why the entry balances.

Sales and purchases

Separate the customer or supplier, the income or expense, and any tax ledger involved in the practice case.

Payments and receipts

Follow the settlement from the party ledger to cash or bank, then inspect the remaining balance.

LEARNING ROUTE

Use one transaction to learn the whole flow.

01

Read the business event

Turn the sentence into a concrete event: sale, purchase, expense, receipt, payment or adjustment.

02

Classify the accounts

Use the account type and the debit-credit rule instead of memorising a disconnected answer.

03

Check the consequence

Inspect the ledger, trial balance and statement effect so the entry has a reason beyond the two columns.

Study habit: after answering, explain the entry aloud in one sentence. If you cannot explain why each account moved, review the source transaction before memorising the format.
COMMON QUESTIONS

Journal entry FAQs

How do I decide which account is debited?

Start with the business event and identify the account receiving value or recording the expense or asset increase. Then apply the relevant account rule and confirm the other side.

Why must total debits equal total credits?

Double-entry bookkeeping records the same event from two linked perspectives. Equality is the first control that the entry has been captured completely.

Can I practise GST entries here?

Yes. Ledgerly includes a GST decision desk that compares same-state CGST and SGST with different-state IGST, but the examples remain educational and configurable.
